Your ads × your flow

Four promises in your ads. One App Store page for all of them.

Meta carries most of the onX Hunt ads we can see: 128 live in the Meta Ad Library on 11 November, in 328 versions, and they promise four different things: when deer will move, whose land it is, better scouting tools and what other hunters are seeing. Every ad that sells the app links to the same App Store page, whose first screenshot is “Know when to hunt”, whichever promise brought the hunter there.

Flow Builder can open a different flow by the campaign the install came from. Below, each of your three biggest app promises gets its own version: its own first question, its own first look at the product and its own membership screen, each judged on the memberships it brings, not the installs.

Day 1

Week 1

One version per promise

Land, forecast and sightings each get their own version; the scouting ads open the land version, on the map.

Week 2

Judge each ad on memberships

Each ad’s flow against today’s, measured on trials, paid memberships and what each install from that ad earns, so spend follows the promise that pays.

Each phone is its own version, and the five ideas above sit underneath all three. In Apple Ads you hold about 97% of US searches for “onx hunt” and 86% for “gps hunting maps”, a strong brand defence, while some spend reaches people who want something else: “google maps” (1%), “waze navigation” (15%), parking searches, “gps manhunt” (92%) and “hunting duel” (56%). That data is account-level, so it can’t tell onX Hunt from onX Offroad. Your Google and YouTube ads are live too; we can’t see where they link. This layer carries no range: only a test per ad can give one.
